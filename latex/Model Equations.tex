\section*{Common conventions and units \cite{BishopVanHorn2023}}

All three multicomponent models are implemented in dimensionless form.  Throughout
this document,
\begin{equation}
    D_i \equiv \partial_i-iA_i,\qquad
    D_t \equiv \partial_t+i\mu,\qquad i\in\{x,y\},
    \label{eq:covariant-derivatives}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the scalar electric potential (the same quantity is sometimes
denoted $\phi$).  Complex conjugation is denoted by an asterisk.  The gauge
transformation
\begin{equation}
    \psi_n\rightarrow e^{i\chi}\psi_n,\qquad
    \mathbf A\rightarrow\mathbf A+\boldsymbol\nabla\chi,\qquad
    \mu\rightarrow\mu-\partial_t\chi
\end{equation}
leaves all of the equations below invariant.

The pyTDGL dimensional scales, in SI, are~\cite{BishopVanHorn2023}
\begin{align}
    \tau_0 &= \mu_0\sigma\lambda^2,
    &B_0=B_{c2}&=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi\xi^2},
    &A_0&=\xi B_{c2}=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi\xi},\\
    J_0&=\frac{4\xi B_{c2}}{\mu_0\lambda^2},
    &K_0&=J_0d=\frac{4\xi B_{c2}}{\mu_0\Lambda},
    &V_0&=\frac{\xi J_0}{\sigma}
        =\frac{4\xi^2B_{c2}}{\mu_0\sigma\lambda^2},
    \label{eq:physical-scales}
\end{align}
where $\xi$ is the coherence length, $\lambda$ the London penetration depth,
$d$ the film thickness, $\Lambda=\lambda^2/d$, $\sigma$ the normal-state
conductivity, and $\Phi_0=h/(2e)$ the flux quantum.  For the single-band,
$d+d'$, and $s+s$ models, solver variables are
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf r=\frac{\mathbf r_{\rm phys}}{\xi},\quad
    t=\frac{t_{\rm phys}}{\tau_0},\quad
    \mathbf A=\frac{\mathbf A_{\rm phys}}{A_0},\quad
    \mathbf B=\frac{\mathbf B_{\rm phys}}{B_0},\quad
    \mu=\frac{V_{\rm phys}}{V_0},\quad
    \mathbf j=\frac{\mathbf K_{\rm phys}}{K_0}.
    \label{eq:dimensionless-variables}
\end{equation}
Order parameters and code coefficients are dimensionless.  The source papers
use Gaussian cgs; all dimensional formulas below are converted to SI, while
dimensionless ratios are unit-system independent.

The $s+d$ equations retain the source paper's order-parameter diffusion clock
$t_\psi=\xi_d^2/\mathcal D$.  Their numerical transport current is therefore
$\mathbf J_{\rm cond}/\beta_{\rm em}$.  Terminal currents are divided by
$\beta_{\rm em}$ on input and solution currents are multiplied by it on output,
so public currents still use the physical scale $K_0$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-scales}.

The $s+s$ equations use two current conversions.  First,
$c_{\rm em}\equiv\texttt{em\_coupling}$ maps the variational condensate current
to the model's transport convention.  Second,
$\beta_{\rm em}\equiv\texttt{beta\_em}$ sets the electromagnetic clock.  The
current stored by the solver and used by the Poisson equation is
$\mathbf j_s=\mathbf J_{\rm cond}/(c_{\rm em}\beta_{\rm em})$.  Terminal
currents are divided by $\beta_{\rm em}$ on input and solution currents are
multiplied by it on output, so the new coefficient is backward compatible at
its default value one.

For each model, current conservation is imposed through
\begin{align}
    \mathbf j_n&=-\boldsymbol\nabla\mu-\partial_t\mathbf A,\\
    \boldsymbol\nabla^2\mu
        &=\boldsymbol\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf j_s
          -\boldsymbol\nabla\!\cdot\partial_t\mathbf A,
    \label{eq:poisson-common}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf j_s$ is the Poisson-normalized supercurrent specified for each
model below.  The $d+d'$ model uses a prescribed vector potential.  The $s+d$
and $s+s$ models can also implement the local electromagnetic evolution given
in their respective sections below.

\section{s+d \cite{Goncalves2014,Zhu1999,Li1999}}

\subsection{Order-parameter equations}

Let $\psi_d$ and $\psi_s$ denote the dimensionless $d$- and $s$-wave order
parameters.  The implemented mixed-symmetry TDGL equations follow the
dimensionless $d+s$ framework of Refs.~\cite{Goncalves2014,Zhu1999}:
\begin{align}
    D_t\psi_d={}&(D_x^2+D_y^2)\psi_d
      +\varepsilon\psi_d-|\psi_d|^2\psi_d
      -\frac{\tau_3}{2}|\psi_s|^2\psi_d
      -\tau_4\psi_s^2\psi_d^*
      +\eta_v(D_y^2-D_x^2)\psi_s,
    \label{eq:sd-d}\\
    \eta_s r_s\,D_t\psi_s={}&\eta_s(D_x^2+D_y^2)\psi_s
      +\nu_{\rm eff}\psi_s-\tau_1|\psi_s|^2\psi_s
      -\frac{\tau_3}{2}|\psi_d|^2\psi_s
      -\tau_4\psi_d^2\psi_s^*
      +\eta_v(D_y^2-D_x^2)\psi_d.
    \label{eq:sd-s}
\end{align}
Here $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathbf r,t)$ is the solver input
\texttt{disorder\_epsilon}.  The effective $s$-sector coefficient is
\begin{equation}
    \nu_{\rm eff}=\nu+c_\nu(\varepsilon-1),
    \qquad c_\nu=\texttt{nu\_disorder\_coupling}.
    \label{eq:sd-nu-effective}
\end{equation}
Thus $\varepsilon$ changes only the $d$ quadratic drive when $c_\nu=0$, which
is the default.  A nonzero $c_\nu$ allows the same spatial profile to model
correlated suppression of both components, as needed for the twin boundaries
in Ref.~\cite{Li1999}.  The standard homogeneous value is $\varepsilon=1$.
The dimensionless $d$-sector stiffness and kinetic coefficient are both one:
Gon\c{c}alves \textit{et al.} use the physical $d$-band stiffness to define
$\xi_d$ and the common diffusion clock $t_0=\xi_d^2/\mathcal D$.  Thus the
$d$ relaxation scale is implicit rather than absent.  The optional positive
factor $r_s$ (\texttt{relaxation\_s}) multiplies only the $s$-sector kinetic
coefficient.  Setting $r_s=1$ recovers Gon\c{c}alves Eqs.~(17)--(18); allowing
$r_s\ne1$ represents a relative $s$-band relaxation time that is independent
of the stiffness ratio $\eta_s$.  The legacy \texttt{Layer.u} coefficient is
used only by the single-band KWT equation and does not set either relaxation
scale in this multicomponent model.

Define the mixed directional currents
\begin{equation}
    Q_i=\operatorname{Im}\!\left(
        \psi_d^*D_i\psi_s+\psi_s^*D_i\psi_d
    \right).
\end{equation}
The condensate current used by the implementation before electromagnetic
normalization is
\begin{align}
    J_{{\rm cond},x}&=
      \operatorname{Im}(\psi_d^*D_x\psi_d)
      +\eta_s\operatorname{Im}(\psi_s^*D_x\psi_s)-\eta_vQ_x,\\
    J_{{\rm cond},y}&=
      \operatorname{Im}(\psi_d^*D_y\psi_d)
      +\eta_s\operatorname{Im}(\psi_s^*D_y\psi_s)+\eta_vQ_y.
\end{align}
The transport-normalized current stored by the solver and used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:poisson-common} is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf j_s=\frac{\mathbf J_{\rm cond}}{\beta_{\rm em}}.
    \label{eq:sd-current}
\end{equation}
The opposite signs of the $x$ and $y$ mixed-current terms encode the
$d_{x^2-y^2}$ symmetry.

The implemented diagnostic \texttt{compute\_s\_plus\_d\_free\_energy}
evaluates the discrete counterpart of the Gibbs functional
\begin{align}
 \mathcal G=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega\!d^2r\,\bigl[&
  -\varepsilon|\psi_d|^2-\nu_{\rm eff}|\psi_s|^2
  +\tfrac12|\psi_d|^4+\tfrac{\tau_1}{2}|\psi_s|^4
  +\tfrac{\tau_3}{2}|\psi_d|^2|\psi_s|^2\\
  &+\tfrac{\tau_4}{2}
    (\psi_s^{*2}\psi_d^2+\psi_d^{*2}\psi_s^2)
  +|\mathbf D\psi_d|^2+\eta_s|\mathbf D\psi_s|^2\\
  &+\eta_v\{(D_y\psi_s)^*D_y\psi_d+\mathrm{c.c.}
               -(D_x\psi_s)^*D_x\psi_d-\mathrm{c.c.}\}
  +\kappa_d^2(B-H)^2\bigr].
\end{align}
For the homogeneous paper model, $\varepsilon=1$; this is Eqs.~(78)--(79)
with the same gauge and normalization conventions as above.

With \texttt{include\_screening=True}, the vector potential obeys
\begin{equation}
    \beta_{\rm em}\partial_t\mathbf A
      =\mathbf J_{\rm cond}
       -\kappa_d^2(\partial_yB,-\partial_xB),
    \qquad B=(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf A)_z,
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~(19) of Gon\c{c}alves \textit{et al.} in the $\phi=0$ gauge.
The implementation writes $\mathbf A=\mathbf A_{\rm applied}
+\mathbf A_{\rm induced}$ and applies magnetic diffusion to
$B-H=\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf A_{\rm induced}$.  This is equivalent to
the paper's $B=H$ vacuum boundary condition for a uniform applied field and
remains variationally consistent for a nonuniform prescribed field.

For an insulating boundary with outward normal $\mathbf n$, the natural
order-parameter conditions generated by the same gradient functional are
\begin{align}
  \mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf D\psi_d
    +\eta_v(-n_xD_x+n_yD_y)\psi_s&=0,\\
  \eta_s\mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf D\psi_s
    +\eta_v(-n_xD_x+n_yD_y)\psi_d&=0.
\end{align}
Substitution into the condensate current of Eq.~(8) gives
$\mathbf J_s\!\cdot\!\mathbf n=0$, precisely Eq.~(12) of Gon\c{c}alves
\textit{et al.}; the finite-element assembly enforces these as variational
natural boundary conditions.

\subsection{Dimensional origin and SI units}

From effective masses $m_d^*,m_s^*,m_v^*$, quadratic coefficients
$\alpha_d,\alpha_s$, quartic coefficients $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_4$, diffusion
coefficient $\mathcal D$, and conductivity $\sigma$, Gon\c{c}alves
\textit{et al.} obtain~\cite{Goncalves2014}
\begin{align}
    \eta_s&=\frac{m_d^*}{m_s^*}, &
    \eta_v&=\frac{m_d^*}{m_v^*}, &
    \nu&=\frac{\alpha_s}{\alpha_d},\\
    \tau_1&=\frac{\beta_1}{\beta_2}, &
    \tau_3&=\frac{\beta_3}{\beta_2}, &
    \tau_4&=\frac{\beta_4}{\beta_2},
    \label{eq:sd-parameter-map}\\
    \beta
      &=\mu_0\sigma\kappa_d^2\mathcal D
       =\frac{\mu_0\sigma\lambda_d^2}
              {\xi_d^2/\mathcal D}.
    \label{eq:sd-beta-map}
\end{align}
$\beta$ is the ratio of the electromagnetic relaxation time
$\mu_0\sigma\lambda_d^2$ to the order-parameter diffusion time
$\xi_d^2/\mathcal D$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-beta-map} is the SI conversion of the
paper's expression.

The remaining source scales are
\begin{align}
    |\psi_{d0}|^2&=\frac{\alpha_d}{2\beta_2}, &
    \xi_d^2&=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_d^*\alpha_d}, &
    \lambda_d^2&=\frac{2m_d^*\beta_2}
        {\mu_0(e^*)^2\alpha_d},\\
    \kappa_d&=\frac{\lambda_d}{\xi_d}, &
    B_{0,d}&\equiv H_{c2,d}^{(0)}=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi\xi_d^2}, &
    \Phi_0&=\frac{h}{2e}.
    \label{eq:sd-source-scales}
\end{align}
SI units are: $m_i^*$ [kg], $\mathcal D$ [$\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$], $\sigma$
[$\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}$], $\xi_d,\lambda_d$ [m], $B$ [T], $\mathbf A$
[$\mathrm{T\,m}$], and $\Phi_0$ [Wb].  Each set
$(\alpha_s,\alpha_d)$ and $(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_4)$ must use common compatible
units; their ratios are dimensionless for any consistent order-parameter
normalization.  $B_{0,d}$ is the nominal pure-$d$ field scale stored by the
code as \texttt{device.Bc2} and denoted $B_0$ by Li \textit{et al.}; it is not
the $\alpha_s$-dependent upper critical field of the mixed state.

\subsection{Mapping the Li--Wang--Wang dynamics}

Li, Wang, and Wang write separate phenomenological kinetic coefficients
$\eta_s^{\rm L}$ and $\eta_d^{\rm L}$ for their fields $S$ and $D$, with
$q\equiv\eta_s^{\rm L}=2\eta_d^{\rm L}$~\cite{Li1999}.  Identifying
$\psi_s=S$ and $\psi_d=D$, first multiply their $S$ equation by two so that
its mixed-gradient term has the same variational coefficient as the $D$
equation.  The static parameter map is then
\begin{equation}
  \eta_s=2,\qquad \eta_v=-1,\qquad \nu=2\alpha_s,\qquad
  \tau_1=\frac83,\qquad \tau_3=\frac{16}{3},\qquad
  \tau_4=\frac43.
  \label{eq:li-static-map}
\end{equation}
Use the $d$-component clock
\begin{equation}
  t_{\rm code}=\frac{t_{\rm L}}{\eta_d^{\rm L}}
              =\frac{2t_{\rm L}}{q}.
\end{equation}
The $D$ kinetic coefficient is thereby one, while the $S$ coefficient is
$2\eta_s^{\rm L}/\eta_d^{\rm L}=4$.  Since the code writes that coefficient
as $\eta_s r_s$, the dynamical map is
\begin{equation}
  r_s=2,\qquad \beta_{\rm em}=\frac{1}{\eta_d^{\rm L}}=\frac{2}{q},
  \qquad
  \mathbf E_{\rm L}=-\partial_{t_{\rm L}}\mathbf A
       =\beta_{\rm em}\mathbf E_{\rm code}.
  \label{eq:li-dynamic-map}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $q$ changes the common physical clock and electromagnetic
coefficient, whereas $r_s=2$ carries the relative $S$--$D$ kinetic factor
that cannot be represented by the static stiffness $\eta_s$ alone.

For a twin profile $g(\mathbf r)$ with
$\alpha_i=\alpha_{i,0}-u_i g$, the script uses
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon=1-u_dg,\qquad c_\nu=\frac{2u_s}{u_d},
\end{equation}
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-nu-effective} gives
$\nu_{\rm eff}=2\alpha_s-2u_sg$.  The ratio is used only for $u_d>0$.

\subsection{Limitations and approximations}

The Gon\c{c}alves formulation treats the diffusion coefficient and conductivity
phenomenologically and does not impose a strict clean- or dirty-limit condition.
The microscopic theory of Zhu \textit{et al.} includes magnetic and nonmagnetic
impurity scattering rather than assuming only the asymptotic dirty limit.
Nevertheless, the implementation's local Ohmic normal current and diffusive
relaxation are dirty-limit-like; quantitative dynamics require material-specific
scattering and relaxation parameters.

By default the field $\varepsilon(\mathbf r,t)$ changes only the $d$-sector
quadratic coefficient; Eq.~\eqref{eq:sd-nu-effective} optionally correlates
the $s$-sector suppression.  This remains a phenomenological rather than a
complete microscopic disorder model.  A scalar
terminal value fixes $\psi_d$ and sets
$\psi_s=0$ at terminal sites, which is a numerical contact model rather than a
general $s+d$ interface condition.

The nominal $H_{c2,d}^{(0)}$ used for nondimensionalization is the pure-$d$
scale.  The mixed-state upper critical field is generally different and must
be computed from the coupled equations, for example from the linearized Li
eigenproblem used by the reproduction script.

\section{d+d' \cite{Lei2000, Wang1999Orbital}}

\subsection{Free energy and equilibrium-relaxation equations}

Let $d$ denote the dominant $d_{x^2-y^2}$ component and $d'$ the
subdominant $d_{xy}$ component.  In the code they are stored as
$\psi_1=d$ and $\psi_2=d'$, respectively.  The dimensionless free-energy
density of Lei, Aruna, and Wang~\cite{Lei2000} is augmented by the optional
orbital-Zeeman coupling derived microscopically by Wang and
Wang~\cite{Wang1999Orbital}:
\begin{align}
    f_{d+d'}={}&-|d|^2-\alpha|d'|^2
      +\frac{1}{2}\left(|d|^4+|d'|^4\right)
      +\frac{1}{3}|d|^2|d'|^2
      +\frac{1}{6}\left(d^*d'+d d'^*\right)^2 \notag\\
      &+|\mathbf Dd|^2+|\mathbf Dd'|^2+\kappa^2b^2
      -i g_Zb\left(d^*d'-d d'^*\right),
    \label{eq:ddp-free-energy}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
    b=(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf A)_z
      =\frac{B_z}{B_{c2}},
    \qquad B_{c2}=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi\xi^2}.
    \label{eq:ddp-field}
\end{equation}
The original field-driven model in Ref.~\cite{Lei2000} deliberately omits the
last term; setting $g_Z=0$ reproduces that functional.  The signed coefficient
$g_Z$ incorporates the microscopic particle--hole asymmetry and the
dimensionless normalization of the orbital-Zeeman interaction.  The code
computes $b$ at mesh sites by a discrete Stokes curl of the edge-centered
applied vector potential.

The source papers specify a static GL functional rather than physical TDGL
kinetics.  The implemented equations are therefore phenomenological negative
gradient flow for finding stationary states:
\begin{align}
    r_dD_td={}&(D_x^2+D_y^2)d+d-|d|^2d
      -\frac{2}{3}|d'|^2d-\frac{1}{3}d'^2d^*
      +i g_Zb d',
    \label{eq:ddp-d}\\
    r_{d'}D_td'={}&(D_x^2+D_y^2)d'+\alpha d'-|d'|^2d'
      -\frac{2}{3}|d|^2d'-\frac{1}{3}d^2d'^*
      -i g_Zb d.
    \label{eq:ddp-dprime}
\end{align}
The factors $2/3$ and $1/3$ follow by differentiating both quartic cross
terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ddp-free-energy}; they are not independent fitting
parameters.

At $b=0$ and $g_Z=0$, a uniform pure-$d$ stationary state has
$|d|=1$ and $d'=0$.  For $1/3<\alpha<1$, the uniform mixed minimum instead
has
\begin{equation}
    |d|^2=\frac{3(3-\alpha)}{8},\qquad
    |d'|^2=\frac{3(3\alpha-1)}{8},\qquad
    \arg(d')-\arg(d)=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}.
    \label{eq:ddp-uniform-minimum}
\end{equation}
For $g_Zb>0$, the Zeeman term lifts this chiral degeneracy and favors
$d'/d=-i|d'/d|$; reversing the field reverses the favored chirality.

\subsection{Current and orbital magnetization}

Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:ddp-free-energy} has equal diagonal stiffnesses and no
mixed-gradient term, the implemented condensate and transport currents are
\begin{align}
    \mathbf J_{\rm diag}
      &=\operatorname{Im}(d^*\mathbf Dd)
        +\operatorname{Im}(d'^*\mathbf Dd'),\\
    \mathbf J_{\rm cond}&=\mathbf J_{\rm diag}+\mathbf J_Z,\\
    \mathbf j_s&=\frac{\mathbf J_{\rm cond}}{c_{\rm em}},
    \qquad c_{\rm em}\equiv\texttt{em\_coupling}.
    \label{eq:ddp-current}
\end{align}
The local dimensionless orbital magnetization associated with the Zeeman term
is
\begin{equation}
    m_z=-\frac{\partial f_Z}{\partial b}\bigg|_{d,d'}
       =i g_Z\left(d^*d'-d d'^*\right),
    \qquad f_Z=-i g_Zb\left(d^*d'-d d'^*\right),
    \label{eq:ddp-magnetization}
\end{equation}
This is the order-parameter orbital contribution and excludes the derivative
of the separately prescribed $\kappa^2b^2$ field energy.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ddp-magnetization} is exposed as the diagnostic
\texttt{Solution.\allowbreak orbital\_\allowbreak magnetization}.
The bound current $\mathbf J_Z$ is evaluated as the exact discrete derivative
of $-\int m_zb\,d^2r$ with respect to the edge vector potential.  It is included
in the current used by the transport Poisson solve.

The discrete condensate free-energy diagnostic includes the local potential,
gradient energy, and orbital-Zeeman energy in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ddp-free-energy}.  It omits $\kappa^2b^2$, which is constant with
respect to $d,d'$ when $\mathbf A$ is prescribed and therefore does not affect
order-parameter relaxation.

\subsection{Limitations and approximations}

Our equations are weak-coupling microscopic results based on BCS, Gor'kov, and path-integral
methods without impurity scattering; they do not assume the dirty limit.  The
coefficients $r_d,r_{d'}$ are phenomenological additions, so the simulated
trajectory is equilibrium relaxation rather than a microscopic clean-limit
TDGL prediction.

The Zeeman term is optional because it changes the phase diagram qualitatively:
when $g_Zb\ne0$, it acts as a linear source for the opposite component and
removes the exact degeneracy of the two chiralities.  Comparisons with the
phase boundary of Ref.~\cite{Lei2000} must use $g_Z=0$; nonzero values instead
implement the microscopic extension of Ref.~\cite{Wang1999Orbital}.

The model has equal component stiffnesses, no mixed-gradient term, and fixed
quadratic coefficients, so nonuniform \texttt{disorder\_epsilon} is unsupported.
Transport terminals require \texttt{terminal\_psi=None} and natural
order-parameter boundary conditions.

\section{s+s and s+is \cite{Zhitomirsky2004,Kogan2011,Lin2016}}

\subsection{Free energy and order-parameter equations}

Let $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ denote the two dimensionless isotropic $s$-wave
components.  Define
\begin{align}
  k_2&=k_{2/1}\equiv\texttt{k2\_over\_k1},&
  k_{12}&\equiv\texttt{mixed\_gradient\_k12},\\
  \gamma_J&\equiv\texttt{josephson\_gamma},&
  \gamma_2&\equiv\texttt{phase\_gamma2},&
  \gamma_3&\equiv\texttt{density\_gamma3}.
\end{align}
The two components share the solver disorder profile $\varepsilon(\mathbf r,t)$,
but have independently tunable responses
\begin{equation}
  a_{i,{\rm eff}}(\mathbf r,t)
    =a_i+c_i[1-\varepsilon(\mathbf r,t)],
  \qquad
  c_1\equiv\texttt{disorder\_coupling1},\quad
  c_2\equiv\texttt{disorder\_coupling2}.
  \label{eq:ss-disorder}
\end{equation}
A positive $c_i$ therefore suppresses component $i$ where $\varepsilon<1$.
With $k_1=1$, the implemented dimensionless free-energy density is
\begin{align}
  f={}&a_{1,{\rm eff}}|\psi_1|^2+a_{2,{\rm eff}}|\psi_2|^2
    +\frac{b_1}{2}|\psi_1|^4+\frac{b_2}{2}|\psi_2|^4\\
  &-\gamma_J(\psi_1\psi_2^*+\psi_2\psi_1^*)
    +\frac{\gamma_3}{2}|\psi_1|^2|\psi_2|^2
    +\frac{\gamma_2}{2}
       (\psi_1^{*2}\psi_2^2+\psi_2^{*2}\psi_1^2)\\
  &+|\mathbf D\psi_1|^2+k_2|\mathbf D\psi_2|^2
    +k_{12}\left[(\mathbf D\psi_1)^*\!\cdot\mathbf D\psi_2
                  +(\mathbf D\psi_2)^*\!\cdot\mathbf D\psi_1\right].
    \label{eq:ss-free-energy}
\end{align}
The first line and the bilinear term give the minimal two-band model of
Refs.~\cite{Zhitomirsky2004,Kogan2011}.  The $\gamma_2$, $\gamma_3$, and
$k_{12}$ terms add the phase-sensitive quartic, density, and isotropic
mixed-gradient couplings used for the $s+is$ model of Ref.~\cite{Lin2016}.

The implemented dissipative extension is the negative gradient flow of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ss-free-energy}:
\begin{align}
    r_1D_t\psi_1={}&(D_x^2+D_y^2)\psi_1
      +k_{12}(D_x^2+D_y^2)\psi_2
      -a_{1,{\rm eff}}\psi_1-b_1|\psi_1|^2\psi_1
      -\frac{\gamma_3}{2}|\psi_2|^2\psi_1
      -\gamma_2\psi_2^2\psi_1^*+\gamma_J\psi_2,
    \label{eq:ss-1}\\
    r_2D_t\psi_2={}&k_2(D_x^2+D_y^2)\psi_2
      +k_{12}(D_x^2+D_y^2)\psi_1
      -a_{2,{\rm eff}}\psi_2-b_2|\psi_2|^2\psi_2
      -\frac{\gamma_3}{2}|\psi_1|^2\psi_2
      -\gamma_2\psi_1^2\psi_2^*+\gamma_J\psi_1.
    \label{eq:ss-2}
\end{align}
The variational condensate current, transport current, and Poisson-normalized
current are
\begin{align}
    \mathbf J_{\rm cond}
      &=\operatorname{Im}(\psi_1^*\mathbf D\psi_1)
        +k_2\operatorname{Im}(\psi_2^*\mathbf D\psi_2)\\
      &\quad+k_{12}\operatorname{Im}
        (\psi_1^*\mathbf D\psi_2+\psi_2^*\mathbf D\psi_1),\\
    \mathbf J_{\rm transport}&=\frac{\mathbf J_{\rm cond}}{c_{\rm em}},&
    \mathbf j_s&=\frac{\mathbf J_{\rm transport}}{\beta_{\rm em}}.
    \label{eq:ss-current}
\end{align}
A positive $\gamma_J$ minimizes the energy for equal component phases; a
negative value favors a relative phase of $\pi$.  When $\gamma_J=0$, a
positive $\gamma_2$ favors the time-reversal-related relative phases
$\arg(\psi_2/\psi_1)=\pm\pi/2$ of an $s+is$ state.

With \texttt{include\_screening=True}, local electromagnetic relaxation uses
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{\rm em}\partial_t\mathbf A
    =\mathbf J_{\rm cond}
      -\kappa^2(\partial_yB,-\partial_xB),
  \qquad B=(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf A)_z.
  \label{eq:ss-screening}
\end{equation}
The implementation evolves $B-H$ with the boundary condition $B=H$.  Local
screening requires $c_{\rm em}=1$ so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:ss-screening} is the
variation of the same free energy as Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ss-1}--\eqref{eq:ss-2}.
Prescribed-vector-potential simulations retain arbitrary positive
$c_{\rm em}$.  In both modes, stored currents use Eq.~\eqref{eq:ss-current};
public physical currents restore the factor $\beta_{\rm em}$.

The variational insulating-boundary conditions are coupled when $k_{12}\ne0$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf D\psi_1
    +k_{12}\mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf D\psi_2&=0,\\
  k_2\mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf D\psi_2
    +k_{12}\mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf D\psi_1&=0.
\end{align}

\subsection{Dimensional origin and SI units}

The minimal Kogan--Schmalian coefficients have the microscopic clean-limit
form below.  A phenomenological SI functional that also includes the
Ref.~\cite{Lin2016} couplings is
\begin{align}
    F_{\rm SI}={}&\int dV\,\biggl\{
      \sum_{\nu=1}^{2}\left[
        a_\nu^{(G)}|\Delta_\nu|^2
        +\frac{b_\nu^{(G)}}{2}|\Delta_\nu|^4
        +K_\nu^{(G)}|\boldsymbol\Pi\Delta_\nu|^2
      \right]
      -\gamma_J^{(G)}(\Delta_1\Delta_2^*+\Delta_2\Delta_1^*)\\
      &+\frac{\Gamma_3^{(G)}}{2}|\Delta_1|^2|\Delta_2|^2
       +\frac{\Gamma_2^{(G)}}{2}
         (\Delta_1^{*2}\Delta_2^2+\Delta_2^{*2}\Delta_1^2)\\
      &+K_{12}^{(G)}\left[(\boldsymbol\Pi\Delta_1)^*\!\cdot
                              \boldsymbol\Pi\Delta_2+\mathrm{c.c.}\right]
      +\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}
    \biggr\},
    \label{eq:ss-dimensional-si}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol\Pi=\boldsymbol\nabla+2\pi i\mathbf A/\Phi_0$,
$\Phi_0=h/(2e)$, and $\Delta_\nu$ is the dimensional band gap.  Superscript
$(G)$ labels dimensional GL coefficients, not powers.

For the clean, isotropic weak-coupling model, the paper gives
~\cite{Kogan2011}
\begin{align}
    a_\nu^{(G)}
      &=\frac{N(0)}{\eta_\lambda}
        \left(S_\nu-n_\nu\eta_\lambda\tau\right),&
    b_\nu^{(G)}&=\frac{N(0)}{W^2}n_\nu,\\
    \gamma_J^{(G)}&=\frac{N(0)}{\eta_\lambda}\lambda_{12},&
    K_\nu^{(G)}&=\frac{N(0)\hbar^2v_\nu^2}{6W^2}n_\nu,
    \label{eq:ss-microscopic-coefficients}\\
    \eta_\lambda&=\lambda_{11}\lambda_{22}-\lambda_{12}^2,&
    S_1&=\lambda_{22}-n_1\eta_\lambda S,\qquad
    S_2=\lambda_{11}-n_2\eta_\lambda S,\\
    W^2&=\frac{8\pi^2(k_BT_c)^2}{7\zeta(3)},&
    S&=\ln\!\left(\frac{2e^{\gamma_E}\hbar\omega_D}
                         {\pi k_BT_c}\right),\qquad
    \tau=1-\frac{T}{T_c}.
\end{align}
Here $N(0)$ is the total single-spin density of states at the Fermi level;
$n_\nu=N_\nu/N(0)$ the band fraction; $\lambda_{\nu\mu}$ the dimensionless
pairing matrix; $v_\nu$ the Fermi velocity; $\omega_D$ the cutoff frequency;
and $\gamma_E$ Euler's constant.  Equations~\eqref{eq:ss-microscopic-coefficients}
are one weak-coupling realization; Eq.~\eqref{eq:ss-dimensional-si} also
applies phenomenologically.

SI units are: $\Delta_\nu,W,k_BT_c$ [J], $N(0)$
[$\mathrm{J^{-1}m^{-3}}$], $a_\nu^{(G)},\gamma_J^{(G)}$
[$\mathrm{J^{-1}m^{-3}}$], $b_\nu^{(G)},\Gamma_2^{(G)},\Gamma_3^{(G)}$
[$\mathrm{J^{-3}m^{-3}}$], $K_\nu^{(G)},K_{12}^{(G)}$
[$\mathrm{J^{-1}m^{-1}}$], $v_\nu$ [$\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$], $\mathbf A$
[$\mathrm{T\,m}$], $B$ [T], and $F_{\rm SI}$ [J].
$n_\nu,\lambda_{\nu\mu},\eta_\lambda,S,\tau$ are dimensionless.

With gap scale $\Delta_0$, length $\xi$, and energy-density scale
$f_0=K_1^{(G)}\Delta_0^2/\xi^2$, the code coefficients are
\begin{align}
    a_\nu&=\frac{a_\nu^{(G)}\xi^2}{K_1^{(G)}},&
    b_\nu&=\frac{b_\nu^{(G)}\Delta_0^2\xi^2}{K_1^{(G)}},\\
    k_{2/1}&=\frac{K_2^{(G)}}{K_1^{(G)}},&
    k_{12}&=\frac{K_{12}^{(G)}}{K_1^{(G)}},\\
    \gamma_J&=\frac{\gamma_J^{(G)}\xi^2}{K_1^{(G)}},&
    \gamma_{2,3}&=\frac{\Gamma_{2,3}^{(G)}\Delta_0^2\xi^2}
                         {K_1^{(G)}}.
    \label{eq:ss-code-map}
\end{align}
If $a_{i,{\rm eff}}^{(G)}=a_i^{(G)}+c_i^{(G)}(1-\varepsilon)$,
then $c_i=c_i^{(G)}\xi^2/K_1^{(G)}$ in the same scaling.
Other scales are equivalent if the whole functional is rescaled consistently.

\subsection{Paper-aligned $s+is$ example}

Ref.~\cite{Lin2016} does not provide a complete nondimensionalization into the
repository's variables.  Assuming the code's band-1 $K_1$/$\xi_1$/$\Delta_0$
scaling, its illustrative mass choice $m_1=m_c=1$, $m_2=2$ maps to
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &a_1=a_2=-1,\qquad b_1=b_2=1,\qquad
  k_{2/1}=\frac{m_1}{m_2}=0.5,\\
  &\gamma_J=0,\qquad \gamma_2=0.5,\qquad \gamma_3=0,\qquad
  k_{12}=\frac{m_1}{2m_c}=0.5.
\end{split}
\label{eq:ss-lin-example}
\end{equation}
These values favor the two $s+is$ relative phases $\pm\pi/2$.  If one
additionally assumes equal physical diffusion constants, the band-1 clock
would give $r_2=0.5$; that is an extra dynamical assumption, not a value
specified by Ref.~\cite{Lin2016}.

\subsection{Lin--Maiti--Chubukov defect comparison}

The reproducibility driver
\path{my_scripts/reproduce_lin_maiti_chubukov_prb94_064519.py} targets
Figs.~3 and 4 of Ref.~\cite{Lin2016}.  They are the most convenient tests of
the distinction because both use the same GL coefficients, require no vortex
initial condition, and test complementary predictions: the weak-defect power
law and the spatial symmetry of the induced field.  In both models the script
uses
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_1=\alpha_2=-1,\quad \beta_1=\beta_2=1,\quad
 \gamma_2=0.5,\quad \gamma_1=\gamma_3=0,\quad
 m_1=m_c=1,\quad m_2=2.
 \label{eq:lin-common-parameters}
\end{equation}
The $s+is$ code map is Eq.~\eqref{eq:ss-lin-example}, augmented by
$c_1=c_2=1$.  With a defect indicator $\chi_D$ and strength
$\widetilde\alpha$, the common solver profile
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon=1-\widetilde\alpha\chi_D
 \label{eq:lin-defect-profile}
\end{equation}
then gives $a_{i,{\rm eff}}=-1+\widetilde\alpha\chi_D$, exactly the
paper's quadratic-coefficient defect.

The repository stores the $s+d$ fields as $\psi_1=\psi_s$ and
$\psi_2=\psi_d$ and uses the $d$-sector stiffness and coherence length as its
units.  The corresponding $s+id$ parameters are
\begin{equation}
 \eta_s=2,\qquad \eta_v=-1,\qquad \nu=1,\qquad
 \tau_1=1,\qquad \tau_3=0,\qquad \tau_4=0.5,\qquad c_\nu=1.
 \label{eq:lin-sid-map}
\end{equation}
The negative $\eta_v$ converts the code's $y$-minus-$x$ directional
convention to the paper's $x$-minus-$y$ convention.  Equation
\eqref{eq:lin-defect-profile} makes both $s+id$ quadratic drives equal to
$1-\widetilde\alpha\chi_D$.  Since
$\xi_1/\xi_d=\sqrt{m_2/m_1}=\sqrt2$, comparison in the paper's units requires
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf r_{\rm code}=\sqrt2\,\frac{\mathbf r_{\rm paper}}{\xi_1},
 \qquad
 \frac{B_{\rm paper}}{H_{c2,1}}=2\frac{B_{\rm code}}{B_{c2,d}}.
 \label{eq:lin-sid-unit-map}
\end{equation}
Both models are initialized in one homogeneous chiral minimum,
$\psi_1=\sqrt2$ and $\psi_2=i\sqrt2$; changing the chirality reverses the
field without changing the reported magnitude.

For the Fig.~3 analogue, the square-defect indicator is
\begin{equation}
 \chi_D=\Theta(r_0-|x|)\Theta(r_0-|y|),
 \qquad r_0=1.5,
\end{equation}
and the script fits $\max|B-B_0|=C\widetilde\alpha^p$ on a logarithmic
strength scan.  The expected signatures are $p=2$ for $s+is$ and $p=1$ for
$s+id$.  For the Fig.~4 analogue,
\begin{equation}
 \chi_D=\Theta(r_0-|x|)\Theta(r_0-|y|),\qquad
 r_0=1,\qquad \widetilde\alpha=0.5,
\end{equation}
which yields the fourfold corner pattern of $s+is$ and the twofold pattern of
$s+id$.  Each field-map panel uses its own symmetric color range because the
absolute amplitudes can differ by orders of magnitude.

The driver uses a $D_4$-symmetric triangular mesh, temporal gauge, local
screening, and subtraction of the identically discretized zero-defect field
$B_0$.  It takes $\beta_{\rm em}=r_1=r_2=r_s=1$ as a numerical relaxation
convention; these kinetic choices do not change the static extrema.
Ref.~\cite{Lin2016} does not specify the finite domain, mesh, boundary
conditions, diffusion constants, conductivity, or penetration depth.  The
script therefore takes $\lambda/\xi_1=4$ by default (and
$\kappa_d=4\sqrt2$ for $s+id$) and presents a finite-domain reproduction of
the symmetry and scaling laws rather than a pixel-level prediction of the
absolute field.  The three resolution presets can be run as
\begin{verbatim}
SCRIPT=my_scripts/reproduce_lin_maiti_chubukov_prb94_064519.py
python "$SCRIPT" --preset smoke
python "$SCRIPT" --preset quick
python "$SCRIPT" --preset paper
\end{verbatim}
Existing HDF5 checkpoints are reused; \texttt{--plot-only} redraws their
figures and \texttt{--force} recomputes the selected cases.

The validation conditions are
\begin{align}
    &b_1>0,\qquad b_2\geq0,\qquad k_2>0,\qquad k_{12}^2<k_2,\\
    &r_1>0,\qquad r_2>0,\qquad c_{\rm em}>0,\qquad
      \beta_{\rm em}>0.
\end{align}
For $b_2>0$, quartic coercivity additionally requires
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\gamma_3}{2}-|\gamma_2|>-\sqrt{b_1b_2}.
\end{equation}
For the passive-band case $b_2=0$, the stricter conditions are $a_2>0$ and
$\gamma_3/2-|\gamma_2|\geq0$.
$a_1,a_2$ may have either sign; an uncoupled uniform band with $a_n<0$ has
$|\psi_n|=\sqrt{-a_n/b_n}$.

\subsection{Limitations and approximations}

The microscopic coefficient map in Ref.~\cite{Kogan2011} is derived for a
clean, isotropic, weak-coupling material, not the dirty limit.  The GL result
that the two gaps are proportional near the common $T_c$ is more general and
also has dirty-limit derivations, but those dirty microscopic coefficients are
not encoded here.  The relaxation coefficients $r_1,r_2$ remain
phenomenological.

Equation~\eqref{eq:ss-disorder} is a phenomenological, linear response of both
quadratic coefficients to one shared scalar profile.  With $c_1=c_2=1$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lin-defect-profile}, it does reproduce the phenomenological
$\alpha_i\mapsto\alpha_i+\widetilde\alpha\chi_D$ defect used by
Ref.~\cite{Lin2016}.  It does not represent a microscopic or multiorbital
impurity potential, nor does it encode impurity-induced interband scattering.
The new quartic and mixed-gradient coefficients are independent
phenomenological inputs unless a consistent microscopic mapping is supplied.

Near $T_c$, the two stored fields do not imply two independent GL coherence
lengths.  The relaxation coefficients remain phenomenological, and the local
screening equation is a dissipative equilibrium-finding dynamics rather than
a material-specific electrodynamic model.  Scalar terminal order-parameter
values remain unsupported because their two-component mapping is unspecified.
